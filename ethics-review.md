Ethics Review – AuraLens

1. Introduction

AuraLens is an AI-powered educational assistant designed to introduce students to the process of screening for dry eye symptoms.

The assistant asks 12 questions about eye comfort and symptoms experienced during the previous week. It collects the user’s responses, calculates a screening score, and provides an educational explanation of the result.

This ethics review examines the project’s main ethical considerations, including privacy, transparency, fairness, safety, accountability, and the limitations of AI in healthcare.

2. Privacy and Data Protection

AuraLens collects information about users’ reported ocular symptoms and eye comfort.

Since symptom information may be considered personal health data, privacy must be carefully protected.

The following principles should be followed:

* Users should not be asked to provide their full name, address, telephone number, or other identifying information.
* Only information necessary for the educational screening process should be collected.
* Users should be informed about the purpose of the assistant and how their responses are used.
* Test scenarios should preferably use fictional or anonymized data.
* Access to collected information should be restricted appropriately.

Any real-world deployment involving personal health data must comply with applicable data protection requirements, including the GDPR.

3. Bias and Fairness

AI systems may produce inconsistent results depending on the information provided by users and the limitations of the underlying model.

AuraLens should treat all users equally and avoid assumptions based on age, gender, lifestyle, or other personal characteristics unless these factors are explicitly relevant to the screening method.

Questions should be clear, neutral, and understandable. The assistant should not interpret missing or ambiguous answers as confirmed symptoms.

The screening score should be calculated consistently using the defined scoring rules rather than subjective AI interpretation.

4. Transparency and Explainability

Users must understand that AuraLens is an educational AI assistant, not a medical diagnostic system.

The assistant should clearly communicate:

* Its educational purpose.
* The fact that it collects self-reported symptoms.
* How the screening score is calculated and interpreted.
* The limitations of the result.
* The importance of professional assessment when symptoms persist or worsen.

The assistant should not claim that its results are clinically validated unless this has been independently demonstrated.

The 12-question screening process must not be confused with the official Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) unless the authorized questionnaire and scoring method are used with the appropriate permissions.

5. Accountability and Human Oversight

AI-generated results may be incomplete or inaccurate. Therefore, responsibility for clinical assessment must remain with qualified healthcare professionals.

AuraLens must not:

* Diagnose dry eye disease.
* Prescribe medication or treatment.
* Recommend changes to prescribed therapies.
* Replace an optometric or ophthalmological examination.
* Provide false reassurance based on a low screening score.

Users reporting persistent or severe symptoms should be encouraged to seek professional evaluation. Sudden vision loss or severe eye pain requires urgent medical assessment.

6. Potential Risks and Limitations

The main risks associated with AuraLens include:

* Misinterpretation: Users may mistake an educational screening score for a medical diagnosis.
* Inaccurate responses: Incomplete or misunderstood answers may affect the final result.
* Scoring errors: Incorrect calculation rules may produce misleading interpretations.
* Overreliance on AI: Students or users may give excessive importance to automated results.
* Privacy risks: Symptom information may be sensitive and should be handled appropriately.
* Limited clinical information: The assistant cannot perform examinations or objective tests of the ocular surface and tear film.

These risks should be reduced through clear instructions, transparent scoring rules, appropriate testing, and human professional oversight.

7. Ethical Conclusion

AuraLens demonstrates the educational potential of Artificial Intelligence in optometry, particularly in learning how to collect and interpret patient-reported symptoms.

Its development must be guided by privacy protection, fairness, transparency, accuracy, and accountability.

The assistant should always be presented as an educational screening prototype. Its results must not replace clinical judgment, professional assessment, or established diagnostic procedures.

Responsible use of AuraLens can help students develop a better understanding of dry eye symptoms while encouraging a critical approach to the opportunities and limitations of AI in healthcare.
