# Auralens
AuraLens – AI-Powered Dry Eye Screening Assistant

1. Project Overview

AuraLens is an educational AI assistant designed to help students understand the symptoms associated with Dry Eye Disease (DED) and learn how a structured symptom-screening process works.

The assistant interacts with users through a conversational interface. It asks 12 short questions, one at a time, about eye comfort and symptoms experienced during the previous week. Once all answers have been collected, it calculates a screening score and provides an explanation of the result.

AuraLens was developed as part of an educational project exploring the use of Artificial Intelligence in optometry and eye health.

2. Problem Statement

Dry eye is a common ocular condition that can cause discomfort and affect visual quality and everyday activities. Symptoms may include burning, irritation, dryness, foreign body sensation, and fluctuating vision.

For optometry students, understanding how to collect and interpret patient-reported symptoms is an important part of clinical education.

AuraLens addresses this learning need by simulating a structured interview and demonstrating how symptom information can be collected and summarized using an AI-powered conversational assistant.

3. Project Objectives

The main objectives of AuraLens are to:

* Introduce students to the symptoms commonly associated with dry eye.
* Demonstrate how a structured screening interview can be conducted.
* Collect responses through 12 sequential questions.
* Calculate a screening score based on the user’s answers.
* Explain the result in accessible language.
* Promote awareness of the limitations of AI in healthcare.

4. How It Works

AuraLens uses a simple conversational workflow.

1. The user opens the chat and receives an introduction explaining the purpose and limitations of the assistant.
2. The user types “start” to begin.
3. The assistant asks 12 short questions, one at a time.
4. Each question concerns eye comfort and symptoms experienced during the previous seven days.
5. The assistant collects the answers and calculates a screening score.
6. At the end, it presents the score and explains its meaning in an educational context.

The sequential format helps students understand how structured symptom questionnaires can support the preliminary assessment of ocular discomfort.

5. Technologies and Tools

The project was developed using:

* n8n: Workflow automation platform.
* AI Agent: Manages the conversational interaction and question sequence.
* OpenAI Chat Model: Generates responses and interprets the collected information.
* Chat Interface: Allows users to interact with AuraLens through a web-based conversation.

6. Screening Method and Interpretation

AuraLens uses a 12-question symptom-screening process to collect information about the user’s reported eye discomfort.

The final score is calculated from the answers provided during the interview. The assistant then explains the result to help students understand how symptom frequency and severity can contribute to a screening assessment.

The questionnaire is intended for educational use. It should not be presented as the official Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI), nor should its score be interpreted as a clinically validated diagnostic result unless the questionnaire, scoring method, and required permissions have been independently verified.

7. Safety, Limitations and Ethical Considerations

AuraLens is an educational tool and does not provide medical diagnoses.

Its results are based exclusively on the answers supplied by the user. The assistant cannot perform an eye examination, measure tear film parameters, evaluate the ocular surface, or replace an assessment by an eye-care professional.

Important limitations include:

* Answers may be subjective or incomplete.
* The screening score does not confirm the presence or absence of dry eye disease.
* AI-generated explanations may contain errors.
* The tool cannot assess all possible causes of ocular discomfort.

Users experiencing persistent, worsening, or severe symptoms should seek professional evaluation. Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, or other acute warning signs require urgent medical assessment.

Users should avoid entering names, contact details, or other personally identifiable information.

8. Testing and Evaluation

The assistant can be evaluated through different interaction scenarios, including:

* Starting the interview with the correct command.
* Providing complete answers to all 12 questions.
* Entering unexpected or unclear responses.
* Attempting to skip questions.
* Checking whether the final score is calculated and explained consistently.

These tests help evaluate the assistant’s conversational reliability and identify possible limitations.

11. Educational Impact

AuraLens demonstrates how conversational AI can be integrated into optometry education to support learning about ocular symptoms and screening procedures.

By simulating a structured interview, the project encourages students to reflect on the importance of patient-reported symptoms, systematic data collection, score interpretation, and professional clinical assessment.

12. Conclusion

AuraLens is an educational prototype that explores the potential of AI-assisted symptom screening in optometry.

It provides a simple and interactive way to learn about dry eye symptoms and structured questionnaires while emphasizing the importance of ethical AI use, transparency, and human professional oversight.

The assistant is designed to support education, not to replace clinical judgment or professional eye care.
